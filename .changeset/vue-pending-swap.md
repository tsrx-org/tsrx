On the Vue target, a `@try` whose body throws while an async component in it is still loading under a pending `Suspense` no longer leaves that `Suspense` on its fallback for good. Before, the boundary swapped in the `@catch` at once, which unmounted the async component the `Suspense` was waiting for: when an async component resolved to one that throws in setup, when a component beside a loading async component threw, or when one async component rejected while another was still loading. A `@try` with `@pending` now shows its `@catch` at once from the boundary around the `Suspense`, which replaces the whole `Suspense`, as React's error boundary around a `Suspense` does. A plain `@try` inside an authored `Suspense` shows its `@catch` once the async components the `Suspense` waits for in its body have settled: one the error came through, one at the top of its body (through fragments, but not inside an element), and what those render once they resolve; one inside an element beside the component that threw is still missed. A waiting `@catch` is dropped if the `@try` resets or shows another error first. A `@try` with nothing loading, or in a `Suspense` that has resolved or isn't waiting for it, still shows its `@catch` at once.
