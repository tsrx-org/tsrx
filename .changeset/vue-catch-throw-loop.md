On the Vue target, an error from a `@catch` body now goes to the next `@catch` out, or to `app.config.errorHandler` when there is none. React and Preact do the same for an error a `@catch` throws as it renders; they don't send an event handler's error to a boundary at all. Before, `TsrxErrorBoundary` caught it itself and rendered the same `@catch` again with it. When the `@catch` threw again as it rendered, that looped until Vue stopped with `Maximum recursive updates exceeded in component <TsrxErrorBoundary>`, and the enclosing `@catch` never showed. It happened when an async component in a `@try` with `@pending` rejected or the `@pending` body threw and the `@catch` then threw, and when a component the `@catch` rendered threw after the `@catch` had rendered, such as a VDOM component, the content of a `Teleport` or `Suspense`, or a watcher.

An error counts as the `@catch`'s when its path reaches the boundary through a component created after the `@catch` first rendered. That includes an error a component of the `@catch` throws later, from an event handler or a watcher, also right after it calls `reset`: before, the `@catch` showed it in place of the first error, or looped when it threw as it rendered. An error from the body still shows in the `@catch`, also one that comes while or after the `@catch` renders. When it comes while the `@catch` first renders inside an outer `@try`, it now shows in that `@catch` too, not the outer one. A component created right under the boundary after the `@catch` first rendered counts as the `@catch`'s, even when body code created it, so its error goes on rather than showing in the `@catch`. That happens when the body threw as it first rendered and one of its `@if` branches, which go on updating out of sight as before, later renders a component, and when a watcher in a detached effect scope renders one while the `@catch` renders. A `@try` with `@pending` renders its `@catch` from two boundaries, and the compiler now marks the inner one `sharesCatch`, so that the `@catch` runs once and its error goes on from both. A `@catch` that doesn't throw, and its `reset`, work as before.
